Case Study · Working title "Stay"

Security Defense

A tower defense where the player never leaves the security booth. They watch the map through a bank of camera feeds, place defenses by clicking through the monitors, and fund their defenses by killing enemies and betting in a crash style minigame.

Role
Solo developer
Engine
Unity
Language
C#
Genre
Tower defense
Platform
PC / WebGL

Finding the concept

The game's constraint was that the player stays in one place. I kept coming back to tower defense, but scope was the main risk. I wanted something small enough to finish and good enough to have a shot on Steam. Before committing, I tried several directions...

  • Papers, Please-style operator sim: a repetitive task at a control panel, operating on test dummies through camera feeds. It had a strong theme but no reason for the player to be there.
  • Upgrade sandbox: damaging the dummy earns money for new tools. Every new interaction meant new content, so it failed the scope check.
  • Incremental tower defense: upgrading the waves instead of the towers.

I settled on tower defense. It fits a player who can't move, and its core loop is proven by games like Bloons, so the work could go into what made this version different.

Systems I built

Camera-feed "movement"

The player moves by switching camera feeds. The cameras are stored in an array, and UI buttons each point to an index in that array to bring up that feed. It's simple and data driven, adding a new viewpoint means adding a camera to the array and a button that points to it.

Click-through-the-monitor raycasting

Towers are placed by clicking on the in game monitor, not on the world itself, so every click has to travel through two cameras.

Step 1
A raycast from the player's camera hits the monitor surface, which displays a render texture.
Step 2
The hit is converted into a Vector2 texture coordinate, in other words where on the screen image the player clicked.
Step 3
That coordinate is used as the origin of a second raycast fired from the security camera into the level, which finds the point in the world where the tower should go.

Crash betting economy

A plain tower defense wasn't distinctive enough, so I added a risk/reward layer. Players can earn money on an in game betting site whose first and currently only game is Crash: they place a bet, the multiplier climbs, and they have to cash out before it crashes.

Crash point
A hidden crash point is rolled when the bet is placed. It's loosely capped at 100×, any roll above 100× is re-rolled into the 10×–100× range, which keeps very high cash-out targets risky.

What I learned

  • Running ideas through a scope check early kept me from committing to a content heavy design I couldn't finish.
  • A familiar genre lets players learn the loop instantly, so the effort can go into the twist, here it's the monitors and the betting.
